   - Key finding: Data restricted to January (2003–2023) at one Polish station; r > 0.9 confounded by >0.8 lag-1 autocorrelation; best-worst MSE spread is only 0.0057; raw precipitation jumps ignored.

9. `Flood susceptibility prediction in the Indo-China Peninsula (Huang et al. 2026).md`
   - Key finding: Inconsistent R² reporting (0.82 in abstract/conclusion vs 0.96 in 2018 benchmark); target is 21-year mean inundation rate (mostly zeros) without null baseline; static susceptibility map marketed for "early warning" despite zero event timing.

## Synthesis for KMUTNB Seminar & Future Research

A critical pattern has emerged across the 59 reviews:
1. **The Preprocessing/Metric Illusion:** A majority of standalone ML claims of "superiority" over NWP or classical baselines dissolve under scrutiny of:
   - Autocorrelation in the target (Ciszyński)
   - Discrepancy between normalized (NMSE) and unnormalized (MSE) scores (Hussain)
   - Tail winsorization removing the exact hazard of interest (Choubey)
   - Baseline aggregation over non-identical datasets (Sharma)
   - Classification imbalance hiding behind raw accuracy (Sharma, Huang)
2. **The Reanalysis Parasitism Problem:** All leading global AI models (AIFS, GraphCast, Pangu-Weather, FourCastNet) are trained on ECMWF's ERA5 reanalysis. They inherit the physical model biases and observational assimilation errors of IFS/ERA5. When verified against analysis fields, they are partially scored against their own ancestry.
3. **The Physics Baseline Reality:** High-resolution physics-based NWP systems (such as PREVENIR in Dillon et al.) explicitly acknowledge their limits (e.g. losing to optical/radar nowcasting at leads < 2 h) and employ object-based metrics (FSS, MODE). ML papers routinely omit nowcasting baselines and rely on mean-reverting point metrics (RMSE/MAE).
