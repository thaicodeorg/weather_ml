Abstract
Predicting runoff plays a key role in flood warning systems in regions like Kendrapara
district, where major rivers such as the Mahanadi, Brahmani, and Baitarani meet. These
areas pose difficulties to develop a precise flood forecasting model because of their moun-
tainous landscape and frequent flash floods. The Ensemble Framework (EF5) for flash
flood forecasting shows promise as a technique without satellite rainfall data. This re-
search applied the EF5 model to build a flood forecast system for the Kendrapara district,
focusing on zones prone to recurrent flash floods. Several benchmark machine learning
models namely Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), Gradient boosting
machines (GBM) and Long Short-Term Memory Network (LSTM) are also applied for
proving effectiveness of the EF5 model. Results confirmed that the EF5 model performed
well during validationwith the Nash-Sutcliffe efficiency (NSE) and Pearson’s correlation
coefficient (PCC) routinely exceeding 0.82. Notably, spline interpolation yields the best
results, with PCC and NSE values above 0.9,followed by Inverse Distance Weighting
(IDW), which comes in second place with PCC and NSE of approximately 0.8; Kriging
performs the worst, with PCC and NSE of approximately 0.4 to 0.6. Nonetheless, dispari-
ties in efficiency are observed across several interpolation techniques. Furthermore, lead
and lag lengths have a considerable impact on the model’s predicted accuracy. Shorter
lead times correspond to greater PCC and NSE values, which surpass 0.8. However, as
the projected lead time rises, these values rapidly decrease, nearing zero or even becom-
ing negative. Given the EF5 framework’s simplicity and validity in Kendrapara’s tiny
mountainous basins, further study and verification are required to improve flood prediction
skills in this region.

1 Introduction
Amongst the most catastrophic natural hazards worldwide are floods. In the last decade,
flood events have impacted over 2 billion people worldwide, accounting for over half of all
natural disaster related deaths (Alderman et al. 2012; Samela et al. 2016; Le et al. 2021).
An abrupt water rush into a narrow canal or over a slanting surface triggered by an unex-
pected, heavy precipitation event in the upstream hilly regions is commonly referred to
as a flash flood (Roy et al. 2019; Jhong et al. 2024; Zhou et al. 2024; Zhai et al. 2025).
Because of their quick and erratic rise inflow rate sand water levels, flash flood events
have attracted substantial consideration from disaster management officials and riverine
inhabitants in hilly areas (Bisht et al. 2018; Nagamani et al. 2024). Flash floods are dis-
tinct from other flood disasters in that they occur suddenly and quickly (Choudhury and
Haque 2016; Das et al. 2020; Majeed et al. 2023; Ikeuchi et al. 2025). The widespread
adoption of traditional flood protection structural measures was lowered by the increased
frequency of catastrophic flood events driven by climate change (Shah et al. 2018; Hadi et
al. 2020; Kuang and Liao 2020).Globally, the severity andrate of flash floods are increas-
ing due to the intricate consequences of climate change, which have exacerbated extreme
rainfall events (Xiong et al. 2024). Because of this, it is now more important than ever to
establish efficient hazard prevention and mitigation strategies (Hu et al. 2024; Shi et al.
2024; Charpentier-Noyer et al. 2025). One common non-structural measure for forecasting
flash floods and their early warning systems is realtime rainfall runoff modeling (Xu et al.
2024). Many studies and practices consider these models to be among the most accurate
and dependable flood forecasting techniques because they can depict runoff processes with
highly time varying and nonlinear properties (Sun et al. 2018). Since flash floods are more
damaging and differ hydrologically from those in vast basins, the scientific community is
becoming more interested in forecasting and warning about them. Since 2000, floods have
claimed thousands of lives in India; majority of those deaths were brought on by flash
floods. The majority of those flash floods have happened in hilly, unprotected regions thus
far. The absence of hydrological data makes forecasting and flash flood warnings extremely
challenging. Extreme flow into a region that is typically dry or a sudden rise in water level
above a flood threshold are characteristics of flash floods. Though the exact timing varies
globally, flash flood occurrences usually occur minutes to hours following the precipita-
tion event that caused them. Numerous research studies have been conducted on runoff
prediction models, including data driven (Moosavi et al. 2022; Yaseen 2023). Two common
multiple satellite rainfall products that are reasonably good at characterizing rainfall trends
are GPMand TRMM (Feng et al. 2024).

2 Study Area
Kendrapara District is situated in the north eastern coastal region of the Indian state of Odi-
sha, with geographical coordinates approximately between 20.167°N-20.750°N latitude and
86.167°E to 87.116°E longitude (Fig. 1). Bay of Bengal borders it to the east, the Bhadrak
District to the north, the Jagatsinghpur District to the south, and the Cuttack District to the
west. It covers an area of approximately 2774 square kilometers. Flood forecast will be a
significant activity in Kendrapara, which is always under threat of inundation, either from
the inland or the seaboard. This is based on feeding data from strategically located rain
gauge centers and on constant monitoring of the flow of river, which would serve as an
important tool in preventive catastrophe management. The anticipation is based on analyz-
ing rainfall patterns from previous years and river flow rates, then predicting in advance the
possibility and severity of flooding disasters. They can also issue a warning in advance to
the areas most likely to be hardest hit. For such a warning system, it will not mean facilitat-
ing evacuation for at risk area communities; they will be allowed to put in place preven-
tive measures mostly for embankment strengthening and provision of emergency response
teams, to minimize the impact of the flood. The methodological flow chart of this study is

3 Methodology
3.1 EF-5 Model
The EF5 is a hydrologic modelling platform applied for research and operational hydro-
logical forecasting, in hydrology and general schematization. The EF5 model couples a set
of water balance schemes with automatic calibration techniques and kinematic and linear
reservoir wave routing as the river routing scheme implementations. The parameters of
the EF5 model were calibrated using the various evolution adaptive Metropolis algorithms
developed by Vrugt et al. 2008. DREAM is a global parameter optimisation approach using
multi chain Monte Carlo simulation in order to get ideal objective values. Based on thepaper
(Flamig et al. 2020), any water balance model can be made to meet basic specifications and
be implemented in EF5.
Other routing options in EF5 include a lumped routing model, often conceptualised as
a kinematic wave approximation, and a series of linear reservoirs based on Saint Venant
equations for 1D open channel flow. These two concepts are further detailed by Flamig et
al. (2020) in their developer documentation. The EF5 model parameters also fall into two
classes: KW parameters, which are related to water movement from one cell to another,
and CREST parameters, which control the water balance. The parameters are as shown in
Table 2. Further calibration of the EF5 model parameters was based on the application of
the Evolutionary Adaptive Metropolis (DREAM) technique.

those that are simpler to classify are given lower weights. Consequently, these weighted

4.4 Discussion
Because of the unique geographical and climatic conditions in Kendrapara District, which
are prone to frequent flooding, this study aims to address flash flood prediction problems.
Utilizing the EF5 model, which was calibrated to local conditions in Kendrapara, will help
officials and stakeholders obtain timely, accurate flood forecasts. It enables them to function
by establishing warning systems, planning evacuations, and allocating resources to mitigate
the effects of flooding on communities, infrastructure, and livelihoods within the district.
Besides, IDW has been found to perform best amongst various rainfall mapping methods,
and selection of such techniques is crucial for estimating precipitation in Kendrapara Dis-
trict. It provides a clear view of rainfall distribution and maps precipitation values using
IDW. This improves flood forecast accuracy, preparing and responding to the region in time.
These findings bring immense value to the literature and inform flood management and deci-
sion making processes in Kendrapara District. Application of the EF5 model and optimal map-
ping of rainfall help the authorities enhance flood prediction and mitigation capacity, saving lives,
property, and infrastructure in this vulnerable area. In this study, rainfall mapping was assessed
using three approaches across three flood events using the EF5 prediction model. It tested several
mapping methodologies and created a rainfall map optimized for the input of the EF5. A forecast
for long-term data is generally more challenging than a short-term forecast because uncertainty
increases with time. Daily rainfall maps for the period 2021–2023, obtained using three different
mapping techniques will be used as inputs to the EF5 model to predict runoff.

5 Conclusion
This study has presented useful information on the flash flood forecast in Kendrapara, a
coastal district with distinctive water patterns. It shows how the EF5 model may be helpful
in forecasting floods in this sensitive district. The EF5 model uses local maps for developing
a realizable runoff forecast system, which is cross validated using data from water stations.
The results of EF5 model were compared with four benchmark machine learning models,
namely RF, SVM, LSTM, and GBM. Comparison of results showed that the proposed EF5
model for flash flood forecasting showed better performance than the ML models for all
scenarios. The validation shows that the model produces realistic, practical short-term flood
forecasts that indicate peak water flow and timing. In another study, by comparing rainfall
mapping techniques such as IDW, Kriging, and Spline, rainfall could be mapped to give
fine flood predictions using spline maps and supply its distribution in a very precise manner.
This confirms that the model is versatile and reliable for predicting floods in this region, as
accurate flood prediction models are developed using local map data. For the EF5 model,
except forthe Kriging Mapping Method, performance was checked against simulated and
real survey runoff data, yielding very high PCC and NSE scores. This reflects a satisfactory
level of fit between the modeled and observed records and attests to the suitability of the
model for predicting short-term floods in Kendrapara. However, we observe that the perfor-
mance of our model does not hold well while forecasting for longer durations. The cryptic
nature of these dynamics has emphasized the need to develop flood prediction techniques
specific to regions of the coast, such asKendrapara. This has impacts on effective risk reduc-
tion, as a reasonable time window for flood forecasting needs to be achieved (which was
one of the prime findings of this study). The inference is that this study finds the relevance
of developing an integrated flood forecasting system tailored for a coastal district like Kend-
rapara, and that it would be precise and provide lead time. Further efforts need to be made by
the researchers to improve the adaptive ensemble framework, which can further focus atten-
tion on increasing efficiency in forecasting. They can do so by integrating new data sources,
such as higher-resolution satellite imagery and remote sensing data. It will also be useful to
test this framework’s performance and adaptability in other flood prone areas with differ-
ent climates and topography. This strategy will show how effective it is in many situations.
