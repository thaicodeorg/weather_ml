such as knowledge distillation ( Hinton et al., 2015 ) or dynamic reso-

main innovation is to train only a high-resolution module on small, re-

guided by a pretrained, frozen low-resolution global model that provides
the necessary global context. By feeding intermediate features from the
frozen backbone to the high-resolution module, this approach substan-
tially reduces GPU memory and computational costs compared to stan-
dard full-grid training. This efficiency is primarily driven by training on
smaller regional patches rather than the entire high-resolution domain,
combined with the fact that gradients are computed only for the train-
able module while the global backbone remains frozen. During infer-
ence, the regionally trained high-resolution module generalizes, seam-
lessly generating complete, high-resolution global forecasts.
Our results show that DeTI achieves a strong balance of efficiency
and accuracy. The framework trains a 0.25° model in just two days
on eight NVIDIA A100 GPUs, using only a small fraction of the re-
sources required by current state-of-the-art models. At the same time,
DeTI maintains forecast accuracy comparable to models such as Pangu-
Weather and GraphCast. Its full potential is evident in its scalability,
demonstrated by performance on 0.09° resolution data that surpasses
traditional physics-based models. This work provides a practical and
resource-efficient path forward and makes the development of next-
generation, kilometer-scale AI weather models feasible without the need
for massive specialized computing clusters.

cessing steps, refer to Text S2.
2.2. Methods
Our proposed DeTI framework is a dual-branch architecture ( Fig. 1 )
designed to strategically decouple high-resolution training from the
global context. We first describe the components, then the training and
inference workflows.
2.2.1. Architectural components
DeTI consists of two core components: the Global-Context Low-
Resolution Network (GC-LRN) and the Detail-Enhanced High-Resolution
Network (DE-HRN). The GC-LRN represents the frozen context within
the system. It is a pretrained global model whose parameters remain
fixed during the training of DeTI. Its primary function is to process low-
resolution global states and to provide large-scale atmospheric context
that guides subsequent stages of learning like a teacher. The DE-HRN
serves as the trainable learner. It processes small, high-resolution re-
gional patches and learns fine-grained physical details that are condi-
tioned on the contextual information provided by the GC-LRN.

3.2. Forecasting high-impact extreme weather
While global metrics like WRMSE ( Table 1 ) are essential for bench-
marking, the true test of a high-resolution framework is its ability to ac-
curately predict high-impact, localized weather events, where fine-scale
physical details are critical. We therefore evaluated DeTI’s performance
on two distinct and challenging types of extreme weather: tropical cy-
clones and regional heatwaves.

4. Conclusions and future work
In this study, we present the DeTI framework, which removes a
major computational barrier to training global high-resolution weather
models. DeTI achieves a strong balance between forecast accuracy and
efficiency, matching the performance of leading systems while using
only a small fraction of their computational resources. For example, the
0.25° model was trained to full convergence within two days on eight
GPUs. This efficiency comes from DeTI’s two-part design, where a high-
resolution regional model learns fine details under the guidance of a
fixed, pretrained global model. Tests show that using this global guid-
ance is essential for keeping forecasts stable and physically consistent.
Furthermore, while our baseline configuration prioritizes efficiency, our
analysis reveals that maximizing the patch size to the hardware limit can
yield additional accuracy gains, albeit at the cost of increased training
duration.
Beyond benchmark evaluations, DeTI also performs strongly in high-
impact weather scenarios. It shows superior skill in predicting typhoon
intensity and accurately captures the fine-scale spatial structure of heat-
waves, demonstrating its potential for both operational forecasting and
scientific research. These results confirm that the framework is not only
computationally efficient but also capable of representing complex phys-
ical processes across scales.
Building on these findings, future work will focus on improving
the interaction between the global and regional components to achieve
stronger cross-scale coupling and more dynamic information exchange.
Specifically, while the current GCI module mainly performs one-way
context injections, future work will explore bidirectional information ex-
change to achieve stronger cross-scale coupling. Additionally, we intend
to investigate importance-based sampling strategies, such as curriculum
learning. By gradually shifting training focus from uniform sampling to
regions with high meteorological variability or forecast error, we aim
to further enhance the model’s capability in handling extreme events.
Another direction involves developing lightweight fine-tuning methods
that can enhance variable-specific performance while maintaining effi-
ciency. These efforts will help extend DeTI toward unified, kilometer-
scale Earth system prediction. Together, these results establish DeTI as
a practical and scalable foundation for next-generation weather and cli-
mate modeling, enabling high-resolution prediction without reliance on
massive computing infrastructure.
