The NWP based approach entails leveraging the recent advances in
numerical weather predictions (NWP) by forecasting ETo with variables
derived from forecasts of NWP models (Karbasi et al., 2022; Liu et al.,
2020; Zhang et al., 2023). Yang et al. (2019) used forecasted weather
variables from public weather forecasts within the FAO Penman
Monteith (PM) formulation for forecasting of ETo at short to medium
range time scales (Yang et al., 2019). Similarly, Tian and Martinez
(2014) used forecast variables from the Global Ensemble Forecast Sys-
tem (GEFS) for forecasting of ETo (Tian and Martinez, 2014). However,
NWP model forecast variables contain biases that are also reflected in
the ETo forecasts estimated (using the PM method) and hence cannot be
directly used for decision making purposes (Saminathan et al., 2024).
Therefore, various statistical techniques such as Bayesian model aver-
ages and Non-homogenous gaussian regression have been proposed for
post-processing NWP based ETo forecasts (Medina et al., 2018; Tian and
Martinez, 2012).
A recent line of research on NWP-based approaches has employed
only temperature forecasts from NWP models in combination with data-
driven (ML/DL) models to predict ETo (Luo et al., 2015; Zhang et al.,
2023). For example, Zhang et al. (2023) used temperature forecasts
from public weather forecast information for training ML/DL models for
forecasting of ETo in China. However, these approaches rely heavily on
model’s
the ML/DL ability to capture the complex non-linear relation-
ships inherent in the FAO-Penman-Monteith (PM) method using tem-
perature data alone (Alves et al., 2017; Reis et al., 2019; Zhang et al.,
2022). Additionally, the models must correct for biases present in the
NWP-derived temperature forecasts. Since accurate ETo estimation via
the PM method requires multiple meteorological variables, relying
solely on temperature forecasts may reduce predictive skill. To address
this limitation, the present study proposes a novel approach that in-
tegrates persistence-based data with NWP forecasts within an ML/DL
framework for enhanced ETo forecasting. The proposed approach in-
(1–7
volves initially estimating short- to medium-range days) ETo
forecasts from the weather variables provided by NWP models using the
PM formulation. These forecasts are subsequently combined with
time-lagged ETo estimates to generate predictions through ML/DL al-
gorithms. This integration helps correct biases in the NWP model out-
puts by leveraging observed time-lagged ETo values (via a
persistence-based approach) and training the ML/DL algorithms using
both the observed ETo and NWP-based forecasts, resulting in improved
ETo predictions. To the best of our knowledge no studies have merged
the persistence-based and NWP based forecasts within ML/DL frame-
work for forecasting of ETo. Further, comparative assessment of using
NWP based forecasts against the plethora of persistence based ap-
proaches in literature are severly lacking. Therefore, in this study we
also do a comparative assessment of the proposed approach against
NWP based forecasts, and persistence based approaches. This will, for
the first time, allow quantification of the improvement in ETo forecast
performance achieved by using NWP information compared to previous
approaches. Further, the proposed approach is compared with one of the
most widely used NWP bias-correction methods to evaluate its efficacy.
India is a large agricultural nation with extensive agricultural water
requirements. The need for irrigation water strains surface and
groundwater supplies, endangering their sustainability (Gedam et al.,
2023; Roy et al., 2021). Current irrigation practices in the region do
not incorporate forecasts, partly due to lack of information on meteo-
rological forecasts. There is an urgent need for including forecast in-
formation for irrigation optimization at regional and local levels towards
meeting sustainability goals. Some studies recently have reported on
ETo forecasts in India, however have mainly been based on persistence
approaches and do not incorporate NWP information (Mandal and
Chanda, 2023; Patil and Deka, 2017; Rajput et al., 2023; Saggi and
Jain, 2019; Sharma et al., 2022). However, none of the studies on ETo
forecasting in India have incorporated NWP based forecast information.
Therefore, in this study we assess the performance of the proposed
methodology for forecasting of ETo across different climate zones and

2.2. Data used
The meteorological variables required for ETo estimation using the
PM method include temperature maximum (Tmax) and minimum
(Tmin), windspeed (Ws), solar radiation (Rs), and relative humidity
(RH). The above mentioned variables were procured from India Mete-
orological Department (IMD) (https://cdsp.imdpune.gov.in/) and ERA5
reanalysis dataset for the entire study area. Reanalysis datasets are
considered as a proxy for observations and have been extensively used in
ETo forecasting studies (Mandal and Chanda, 2023; Purnadurga et al.,
2019; Saggi and Jain, 2019; Tarek et al., 2020; Varghese and Mitra,
2025; Veysi et al., 2024). Previous research has also highlighted the
reliability of ERA5 and its suitability as a substitute or complement to
observational data, particularly in regions with sparse station coverage
Mun˜oz-Sabater

reanalysis dataset is used due to the unavailability of weather variables
from ground based stations within the study region. Even though a few
stations exist, but the duration of the datasets vary, hindering compre-
hensive analysis and comparison across different regions. Further,
recent studies by Veysi et al. (2024)and Tarek et al. (2020)also reported
that the ERA5 reanalysis dataset showed strong correlation with the
ground measurements. Over the Indian subcontinent, ERA5-derived
variables have been widely used for reference evapotranspiration
(ETo) forecasting (Aadhar and Mishra, 2020; Mahto and Mishra, 2019;
Mandal and Chanda, 2023; Varghese and Mitra, 2024).

The proposed approach was evaluated across different regions and
agricultural seasons in India. The framework was able to provide skillful
forecasts even in regions where the NWP forecast skill was relatively low
(Zone 2). However, the level of skill achieved in these regions was lower
compared to that obtained for the other zones, primarily due to the
inherently lower performance of the NWP forecasts themselves. The
poorest performance of the NWP models is observed in the zone 2, where
both NRMSE and MAE values are notably higher (Fig. 4 and Fig. S2). In
this region, the skill of ETo forecasts is relatively low compared to other
zones, primarily due to the weak temperature forecast skill in the region
which has already been explored in depth by Saminathan and Mitra
(2025) (Saminathan and Mitra, 2025). Previous studies have consis-
tently highlighted the limited accuracy of temperature forecasts in the
Himalayan regions (Z2) (Durai and Bhradwaj, 2014;Saminathan and
Mitra, 2024; Tiwari et al., 2016). For instance, Tiwari et al. (2016) re-
ported substantial winter temperature biases over the Himalayas,
attributing them to the models inability to realistically capture the
complex orography of the region due to limited spatial resolution.
Similarly, Mishra (2015) and Gerlitz et al. (2014) noted that tempera-
ture simulations from climate models exhibit significant uncertainties in
the Himalayan region, largely influenced by elevation variations and
climate models resolution.
Further, forecasts were found to be better for the Rabi season
compared to the Kharif season across the region. The Kharif season
(June–September)
coincides with the onset of the southwest monsoon
and is typically marked by high humidity, elevated temperatures, and
intense rainfall activity. These climatic conditions substantially increase
the atmospheric demand, resulting in higher and more variable refer-
ence evapotranspiration (ETo) values. Such variability poses challenges
for predictive models, as the fluctuations in meteorological parameters
lead to greater uncertainty in ETo forecasting. In contrast, the Rabi
(October–March)
season corresponds to the winter cropping period,
during which temperatures are relatively lower, rainfall is scarce, and
atmospheric demand is subdued. Consequently, the ETo values during
this season are generally lower and more stable, with reduced variability
compared to the Kharif season. The relative stability of ETo during Rabi
enhances the ability of ML and DL models to capture underlying pat-
terns, thereby improving predictive performance. Consistent with these
observations, Mandal and Chanda (2023) also reported that predictive
accuracy tends to be higher under conditions of low and stable ETo (Rabi
season), while performance declines with an increase in ETo magnitude
and variability (Kharif season). This highlights the direct influence of
seasonal climatic variability on the skill of ML/DL-based ETo forecasting
approaches. Further, since ETo by definition is not dependent on the
type of crops sown, therefore the accuracy of the values are not
dependent on the crop themselves.
Overall, we do not find substantial difference between the different
ML/DL models in forecasting ETo. However, few studies in literature
have reported difference in performance of the ML models for fore-
casting ETo though those studies were solely based on persistence ap-
proaches (Karbasi et al., 2022; Mandal and Chanda, 2023; Reis et al.,
2019; Saggi and Jain, 2019). Saggi and Jain (2019) compared four
different ML/DL models such as RF, Gradient-Boosting Machine,
Generalized Linear Model and Deep Learning-Multilayer Perceptrons
and reported that the Deep Learning-Multilayer Perceptrons out-
performs the other ML/DL models in forecasting ETo. Similary Reis et al.
(2019) compared three different ML approaches such multiple linear
regression, extreme learning machine and artificial neural network, they
found that multiple linear regression is best suited for ETo forecasting in
Brazil. Furthermore, we also find that the performance of the proposed
approach is considerably better than those of that of BMA a widely
employed NWP bias correction technique. Many studies (Ahmed et al.,

2020; Barlow et al., 2018) have reported on the performance of such
bias-correction approaches towards forecast enhancement, however we
show that the proposed approach comprehensively outperforms the
BMA approach. Overall, as all of the ML/DL models show similar per-
formance within the proposed framework, the time taken for each model
should therefore determine the choice of ML/DL models in the
framework.
6. Conclusion
Short- to medium-range ETo forecasts are necessary for irrigation
scheduling purposes and agricultural water management. In this study,
we proposed an ETo forecasting framework integrating ML/DL algo-
rithms with NWP forecasts and persistence based approaches. Further,
we also compare different ML/DL algorithms within the proposed
framework. The proposed framework was also evaluated across different
climate zones and agricultural seasons over the Indian regions. The
major conclusions from this study are as follows.
1. The proposed ML/DL-based framework was able to provide better
ETo forecasts than the NWP models, whose forecasts exhibited high
errors (NRMSE, MAE, RMSE) and are therefore unsuitable for direct
end-user application.
2. Comparable performance was observed between the different ML/
DL techniques (SVR, XGB, RF, LSTM and CNN), and no single ML/DL
technique was found to outperform the other.
3. The XGB model demonstrated the lowest computational time among
the evaluated models while achieving comparable predictive per-
formance, making it the preferred approach for future ETo fore-
casting applications.
4. Performance assessment across major crop growing seasons indi-
cated that the proposed framework provided better forecasts for the
Rabi season compared to the Kharif, which is due to the low skill of
the NWP based forecasts for the Kharif season compared to Rabi.
5. Comparison of the proposed ML/DL based framework against BMA
and the persistence-based approach showed that the proposed
framework performs better than both the approaches, especially for
regions having higher NWP forecast skill. Further, the proposed
framework is able to provide better results than persistence based
approach and the BMA even at higher lead times.
The proposed framework based on integrating NWP-based forecasts
and time lagged ETo observations within ML/DL algorithms can be used
for operational purposes and will be helpful for the irrigation and
farming community.
